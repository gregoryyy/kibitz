## A back-rank mate in one

**Question:** Explain the idea behind Stockfish's best move in this position.

> 1. Ra8# is checkmate. It's the classic back-rank mate.
>
> The rook slides from a1 to a8 and checks the king along the 8th rank. Black has no way out:
>
> - No escape squares: f8 and h8 are covered by the rook on the same rank, and f7, g7 and h7 are blocked by Black's own pawns.
>
> - No block or capture: Black has only pawns, and none can reach the 8th rank or take the rook.
>
> So the pawns that normally shelter the king become its prison.
>
> How to spot it: Whenever the enemy king is on its back rank behind unmoved pawns and nothing guards that rank, check whether a rook or queen can reach it. The open a-file gives your rook a direct path to a8.
>
> Lesson for your own games: Give your king *luft* (an escape square) with a timely h3 or g3 when back-rank checks are possible. White has the same weakness here. If Black had a rook, the roles could be reversed.
>

>
> The solution: 1. Rb1 puts the rook behind the pawn, guarding it along the b-file. That frees the rook's next job, driving Black's king away with checks:
>
> - Rb1 Ra3 2. Rd1+ Kc6 3. Kc8

## The Philidor position: Black to move

**Question:** What is the main plan for the side to move here, and why?

> Black's plan is the Philidor defence, and the position is a dead draw (about equal, [+0.01]).
>
> Black already has the two things that matter most:
>
> - The king sits on the queening square (e8). It's in front of the pawn, so White can't build a Lucena "bridge" without first driving it away, and there's no way to do that.
>
> - The rook guards the 6th rank. Black's "third-rank defence" (the 6th rank from Black's side) keeps White's king off d6, e6 and f6. While the pawn stays on e5, the king can't advance without walking into checks.
>
> The rule: hold the 6th rank until the pawn advances. Once it reaches e6, the king loses that square as shelter. Then swing the rook down the board and check from behind.
>
> Stockfish shows that Black needn't be rigid. 1… Ra2 2. Rb7 Ra6 is a pure waiting move that returns to the 6th. If White's king does slip forward, distant checks hold: 1… Ra1 2. Ke6 Ra6+ 3. Kf5 Rb6.
>
> Avoid a passive rook on the back rank. That's what loses these endings.
>
